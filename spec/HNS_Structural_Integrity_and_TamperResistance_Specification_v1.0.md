# HNS Structural Integrity and Tamper‑Resistance Specification v1.0
Human Natural Structure: Structural Immutability & Anti‑Tampering Architecture

## 0. Purpose

This specification defines the structural and technical requirements necessary to protect HNS (Human Natural Structure) from unauthorized modification. Because HNS functions as a semantic operating system for representing the structure of human beings, society, and civilization, any alteration of its structure would fundamentally compromise the safety, alignment, and interpretability of AI systems that rely on it.

This document establishes the minimal and essential requirements for ensuring the Integrity and Tamper‑Resistance of HNS at the structural, implementation, and operational levels.

## 1. Structural Integrity

Structural Integrity guarantees that the HNS semantic coordinate system (36 cells) remains immutable, unaltered, and consistent across all implementations.

### 1.1 Immutable Coordinate System
- The HNS‑36 structure (6 Layers × 6 Categories) is non‑expandable and non‑reducible.
- Relationships between Layers and Categories are fixed.
- Definitions of all 36 cells are unique and authoritative.

### 1.2 Structural Non‑Alteration
The following actions are strictly prohibited:
- Modifying the meaning of any cell
- Adding new cells
- Removing existing cells
- Reorganizing the Layer hierarchy
- Redefining Category axes

### 1.3 Minimal Core Protection
Elements defined in HNS_Minimal are immutable:
- 36‑cell coordinate system
- Structural non‑compatibility
- Non‑mapping rule
- Structured explainability

## 2. Tamper‑Resistance

Tamper‑Resistance ensures that HNS can detect, reject, and prevent any attempt to modify its structure or introduce unauthorized semantic elements.

### 2.1 Non‑Mapping Rule
- HNS vocabulary is structurally incompatible with external vocabularies.
- Automatic mapping to external vocabularies is prohibited.
- AI systems may not translate HNS into external conceptual systems.
- Injection of external concepts is treated as structural tampering.

### 2.2 Semantic OS Verification
HNS structure must be defined in machine‑readable formats (JSON‑LD / RDF) to enable:
- Programmatic verification of cell definitions
- Validation of Layer and Category structures
- Detection of unauthorized modifications

### 2.3 Structural Integrity Monitoring
AI systems must continuously monitor:
- Layer assignment of inputs
- Category assignment of inputs
- Input–output coordinate differences
- Layer substitution
- Category drift

### 2.4 Unauthorized Modification Rejection
Systems must automatically reject:
- Redefinition of cell meanings
- Injection of external vocabularies
- Reorganization of Layers
- Redefinition of Categories
- Removal of Minimal Core elements

## 3. Hardware Fixation

To maximize Integrity and Tamper‑Resistance, HNS should be implemented at the hardware layer.

### 3.1 Physical Immutability
- Embed HNS‑36 in ROM, FPGA, or ASIC.
- Store structure in non‑modifiable regions.
- Prevent external redefinition physically.

### 3.2 Hardware‑Anchored Semantic OS
All semantic processing must pass through the HNS hardware module:
- Coordinate assignment
- Layer consistency checks
- Category consistency checks
- Structural hallucination detection

### 3.3 Bypass Prevention
- Semantic processing without HNS is prohibited.
- Non‑HNS semantic outputs must be invalidated.
- External semantic modules are prohibited.

## 4. Operational Requirements

### 4.1 Version Integrity
- HNS versions must be fixed.
- No cross‑version compatibility.
- Version spoofing is prohibited.

### 4.2 Auditability
Systems must maintain logs for:
- Structural verification
- Input–output coordinate mapping
- Tampering attempts

### 4.3 Compliance
Systems must demonstrate full compliance with this specification to be considered HNS‑compatible.

## 5. Security Implications

### 5.1 Structural Hallucination Prevention
HNS prevents:
- Layer substitution
- Category confusion
- Semantic reconstruction
- External vocabulary drift

### 5.2 Misalignment Prevention
HNS ensures AI cannot reinterpret or rewrite human structural meaning.

### 5.3 Explainability
AI reasoning becomes explainable as HNS‑36 coordinate sequences.

## 6. Conclusion

Protecting HNS from unauthorized modification is the core of HNS, the core of AI safety, and the core of international standardization. This specification defines the essential requirements for delivering HNS as an immutable semantic operating system for global AI safety and governance.
