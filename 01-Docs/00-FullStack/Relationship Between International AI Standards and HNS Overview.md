## 1. Introduction

International AI standards are expanding rapidly. The EU AI Act, ISO/IEC 42001, the NIST AI Risk Management Framework, IEEE ethical standards, and emerging bodies such as IFAIS now form a dense regulatory landscape.

Yet a critical gap persists:

> Standards define *that* AI must be safe, transparent, and aligned with human values —  
> but none defines *how* to structurally measure alignment with human cognition, behavior, and meaning.

Human Natural Structure (HNS) directly addresses this missing layer.

This document explains how HNS aligns with major international AI standards, fills their structural gaps, and provides an implementation pathway for requirements that standards demand but do not define.

---

## 2. Overview of Major International AI Standards

### 2.1 EU AI Act (Regulation 2024/1689)
- First comprehensive AI law.
- Requires transparency, logging, human oversight, and conformance testing.
- Does **not** define what logs should measure or the baseline for oversight.

### 2.2 ISO/IEC 42001 — AI Management System
- Governance and risk management framework.
- Defines processes, not technical verification.

### 2.3 ISO/IEC 23894 — AI Risk Management
- Requires risk identification and mitigation.
- Does not define a structural model of human wellbeing.

### 2.4 NIST AI RMF 1.0
- GOVERN / MAP / MEASURE / MANAGE.
- MEASURE lacks a technical implementation.

### 2.5 ISO/IEC 22989 — AI Concepts and Terminology
- Defines vocabulary.
- Does not define a structural coordinate system.
