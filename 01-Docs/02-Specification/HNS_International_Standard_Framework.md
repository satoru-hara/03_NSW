## 1. Introduction: Overcoming the Global Absence of Meaning Structures in the AI Era

While AI computational capabilities are increasing exponentially, there remains no objective global standard for verifying whether the "meaning" generated by AI aligns with human causal structures.

Existing international standards — ISO/IEC 42001, IEEE 7000, NIST AI RMF, and the EU AI Act — define requirements for transparency, accountability, and process-level risk management. However, they lack a mechanism for evaluating or controlling the **structural validity** of AI outputs.

The **Human Natural Structure (HNS)** framework addresses this gap by defining the invariant structure of human cognition as a **common Structural Operating System (Structural OS)**. This enables **Semantic Synchronization** between AI systems and human reasoning, forming the world’s first **International Standard Framework** for meaning alignment.

## 2. HNS-36: Foundational Specification

HNS-36 is defined as the **Minimal Complete Structure** derived from the Cartesian product of:

- Six Natural Layers (vertical causal hierarchy)
- Six Cognitive Categories (horizontal interpretive axes)

Together, they form a 36-cell coordinate system for representing human meaning, intention, and behavior.

---

### 2.1 Vertical Axis: Six Natural Layers (Causal Depth)

- Physical: Biological substrate, physiology, hardware, physical constraints  
- Perceptual: First-order processing of external information, sensory input  
- Internal: Emotions, memory, internal mental states  
- Intentional: Goals, agency, initiation of decision-making  
- Relational: Interpersonal interaction, dynamic engagement with environments  
- Societal: Institutions, culture, legal norms, civilizational structures  

---

### 2.2 Horizontal Axis: Six Cognitive Categories (Interpretive Perspectives)

- Object (entity)  
- Purpose (reason)  
- Means (method)  
- Condition (context)  
- Constraint (restriction)  
- Value (priority/weight)
