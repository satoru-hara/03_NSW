## 1. Basic Understanding

**Human Natural Structure (HNS) Foundational Specification**  
A specification that organizes the structural principles of human cognition, behavior, social structure, and civilization into a unified framework based on the HNS-36 matrix. The 36-cell coordinate system consists of six natural layers (causal structures) and six abstract cognitive categories (non-causal explanatory axes), aiming to provide a precise positioning for both human thought and AI interpretation.

**On the Origin of Species (Charles Darwin, 1859)**  
A scientific theory that explains biodiversity through the principles of natural selection and the struggle for existence. It clarifies the structural mechanisms of species evolution by comparing variation, adaptation, and survival rates across individuals, populations, species, and ecosystems.

## 2. Points of Structural Similarity

| Comparison Axis                  | HNS Foundational Specification                          | On the Origin of Species                              | Similarity |
|----------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------|------------|
| Hierarchical Structure           | Six Natural Layers × Six Cognitive Categories           | Hierarchical evolutionary structure from individual to ecosystem | Both employ hierarchical analysis from part to whole |
| Explanation of Causal Relationships | Each cell represents the causal origin of human and civilizational phenomena | Natural selection explains evolutionary processes through causal relationships between survival and reproduction | Both provide causal interpretive models |
| Integrated Framework             | Integrates complex phenomena of cognition, behavior, society, and civilization into 36 cells | Integrates intraspecific variation, selective pressure, and adaptation to explain biodiversity | Both organize complex phenomena into a unified explanatory model |
| Purpose of Prediction and Explanation | Predicts behavioral, social, and civilizational changes while guaranteeing structural interpretability of AI | Predicts and explains species distribution, morphology, and behavior resulting from evolution | Both use structural principles for causal inference and future prediction |

## 3. Essential Insights

Both HNS and *On the Origin of Species* share a fundamental methodological approach: they analyze hierarchical, causal, and integrated structures.

While their subjects differ — HNS addresses artificial and social phenomena such as human cognition, society, and civilization, whereas Darwin focuses on biological and natural phenomena — both adopt a **structuralist perspective**. They extract universal laws from observational data and construct comprehensive explanatory frameworks.

The key difference lies in formalization: HNS is a highly abstract, formal coordinate system designed for AI implementation, whereas Darwin’s work is a descriptive theory grounded in natural observation.

## 4. Summary

The commonality between the two lies in their framework of **“abstraction and integrated explanation of hierarchical causal structures from the individual to the whole.”**

HNS can be viewed as a methodological extension of the structural interpretation found in *On the Origin of Species*, applied to the domain of human society, cognition, and civilization.

**Conclusion**

The similarity between HNS and *On the Origin of Species* lies in their theoretical methodologies. Although their subjects differ, both abstract and comprehensively explain the hierarchical causal structures of individuals, groups, and the whole based on observational data. This shared conceptual integration of scientific insight and structural modeling represents a powerful approach to understanding complex systems.
