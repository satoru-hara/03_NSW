# Similarities between the Human Natural Structure Foundational Specification and *On the Origin of Species*

## 1. Basic Understanding

**Human Natural Structure (HNS) Foundational Specification**  
A specification that organizes the structural principles of human cognition, behavior, social organization, and civilization into a unified framework based on the HNS-36 matrix. The 36-cell coordinate system consists of six natural layers (causal structures) and six abstract cognitive categories (non-causal explanatory axes), aiming to establish a unified structural positioning for both AI and human thought.

**On the Origin of Species (Charles Darwin, 1859)**  
A scientific theory explaining biodiversity through the principles of natural selection and the struggle for existence. It clarifies the structural mechanisms of species evolution by comparing variation, adaptation, and differential survival among individual organisms, thereby explaining evolutionary patterns at the levels of populations, species, and ecosystems.

## 3. Essential Insights

Both frameworks share hierarchical, causal, and integrative perspectives. Conceptually, HNS provides a structural operating system for human civilization, while Darwin systematizes the structural principles governing biological evolution.

Although their domains differ, their theoretical approaches are similar:

- HNS addresses artificial and social-scientific domains such as cognition, society, and civilization.
- Darwin addresses biological and natural phenomena.

Both employ structuralist methodologies to extract universal principles from observational data.

A key difference lies in formalization and abstraction:

- HNS employs a highly formalized, abstract model based on a 36-cell coordinate system designed with computational and AI applications in mind.
- Darwin presents a conceptual explanatory theory without mathematical or software-oriented formalization.

---

## 4. Summary

The commonality between the two lies in their shared framework of **abstracting and integratively explaining hierarchical causal structures from individuals to larger systems**.

Their differences arise primarily from domain and implementation:

- HNS addresses AI, cognition, society, and civilization using formal coordinates and structural mapping.
- Darwin addresses biological evolution through descriptive natural laws.

Thus, the Human Natural Structure Foundational Specification can be regarded as a **methodological extension** of the structural interpretation found in *On the Origin of Species*, applying similar principles to human cognition, society, and civilization.

---

## Conclusion

**The similarity between HNS and *On the Origin of Species* lies in their theoretical methodologies: although their subjects differ, both abstract and comprehensively explain hierarchical causal structures—from individuals to groups to entire systems—based on observational data.** The common thread is the conceptual integration of scientific insight with systematic structural organization.
